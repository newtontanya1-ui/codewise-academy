Lesson 7: Pathology and Laboratory

Learn how CPT codes are used to report pathology, laboratory tests, and related diagnostic services.

The Pathology and Laboratory Section

The CPT Pathology and Laboratory section contains codes used to report many laboratory tests and pathology services.

These services can provide important information used to help diagnose, monitor, and manage a patient's condition.

Types of Laboratory Services

The Pathology and Laboratory section includes many different types of services.

Examples include:

  • Organ or disease-oriented panels
  • Drug testing
  • Chemistry tests
  • Hematology tests
  • Immunology tests
  • Microbiology services
  • Surgical pathology

Laboratory Panels

CPT includes certain laboratory panel codes that represent groups of specified laboratory tests.

Coders should review the requirements for the specific panel before reporting a panel code.

Remember: Do not automatically report a panel code simply because several laboratory tests were performed. The requirements for the panel must be met.

Specimens

Laboratory and pathology services often involve examination or testing of a specimen.

Examples of specimens may include blood, urine, tissue, or other materials collected for testing.

The type of specimen and service performed can be important when determining the appropriate code.

Surgical Pathology

Surgical pathology involves the examination of tissue or other specimens removed during a procedure.

The documentation should support the type of specimen examined and the pathology service performed.

Medical Necessity

Laboratory testing should be supported by the patient's medical record and applicable coverage requirements.

Diagnosis information can help explain why a laboratory service was medically necessary.

Important: CPT identifies the service performed, while diagnosis coding helps describe the patient's condition or reason for the service.

Documentation Matters

Accurate laboratory coding requires documentation of the tests or services actually performed.

Coders should review the medical record, laboratory report, CPT code descriptions, and applicable coding instructions.

Example

A provider orders laboratory testing for a patient.

The coder should review which tests were actually performed and determine whether individual codes or an applicable panel code accurately represents the documented services.

Quick Check

Should a coder automatically report a laboratory panel code whenever several tests are performed?

Answer: No. The coder should verify that the requirements for the specific panel are met before reporting it.

Lesson 7 Goals

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

  • Explain the purpose of the Pathology and Laboratory section.
  • Recognize common types of laboratory services.
  • Understand the basic concept of laboratory panels.
  • Recognize the importance of specimens.
  • Understand the basic purpose of surgical pathology.
  • Explain the importance of medical necessity.
  • Understand why complete documentation matters.
