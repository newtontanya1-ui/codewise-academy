Medical Billing Basics

  • Medical billing is the process of submitting healthcare claims to insurance companies.
  • Billing staff use diagnosis and procedure codes to report services provided to patients.
  • Accurate billing helps healthcare providers receive proper reimbursement.
  • Claims must be complete, accurate, and submitted on time.

Common Insurance Terms

  • Premium: The amount paid for insurance coverage.
  • Deductible: The amount a patient pays before insurance begins paying certain costs.
  • Copayment: A fixed amount the patient pays for a covered service.
  • Coinsurance: A percentage of the cost the patient is responsible for paying.
  • Allowed Amount: The maximum amount an insurance plan will pay for a covered service.

The Billing Process

  • Collect and verify patient information.
  • Verify insurance coverage and eligibility.
  • Review provider documentation.
  • Assign the correct diagnosis and procedure codes.
  • Create and submit the insurance claim.
  • Review the payer response.
  • Post payments and adjustments.
  • Follow up on unpaid or denied claims.

Claim Forms

  • CMS-1500: Commonly used for professional and physician services.
  • UB-04: Commonly used for hospital and facility services.
  • Claims should include accurate patient, provider, insurance, diagnosis, and procedure information.

Denials and Appeals

  • A denied claim is a claim the payer has refused to pay.
  • Common reasons include incorrect patient information, coding errors, missing authorization, or lack of medical necessity.
  • Always review the denial reason before correcting and resubmitting a claim.
  • An appeal may be submitted when the provider believes the payer's decision should be reconsidered.

Helpful Billing Tips

  • Verify insurance information before services are provided whenever possible.
  • Double-check patient demographics before submitting a claim.
  • Make sure diagnosis and procedure codes match the medical documentation.
  • Watch claim filing deadlines.
  • Keep track of unpaid and denied claims.
  • Protect patient information and follow HIPAA requirements.
