Lesson 2: ICD-10-CM Code Structure

Learn how ICD-10-CM diagnosis codes are organized and built.

How are ICD-10-CM codes structured?

ICD-10-CM codes use letters and numbers to identify diagnoses, diseases, injuries, symptoms, and other health conditions.

A code can contain between three and seven characters. The first character is a letter, and the remaining characters may contain letters or numbers.

Categories and Subcategories

The first three characters of an ICD-10-CM code identify the category.

Additional characters provide more specific information about the patient's condition.

Example: E11 is the category for Type 2 diabetes mellitus. Additional characters make the diagnosis more specific.

Lesson Goal

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

  • Recognize the basic structure of an ICD-10-CM code.
  • Understand categories and subcategories.
  • Explain why additional characters are important.
