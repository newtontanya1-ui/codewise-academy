Lesson 1: Introduction to CPT Coding

Learn what CPT codes are, why they are used, and how they help describe healthcare services and procedures.

What Is CPT?

CPT stands for Current Procedural Terminology.

CPT codes are used to describe medical, surgical, and diagnostic services and procedures performed by healthcare professionals.

Remember: ICD-10-CM codes generally describe diagnoses, while CPT codes describe services and procedures.

Who Maintains CPT?

The CPT code set is maintained by the American Medical Association, also known as the AMA.

CPT codes are updated regularly, so medical coders need to use the code set and guidance that apply to the date of service.

What Do CPT Codes Look Like?

Category I CPT codes contain five numeric characters.

Example:

A Category I CPT code may look like this:

99213

Coders must review the complete code description and applicable CPT instructions before assigning a code.

Why Are CPT Codes Important?

CPT codes provide a standardized way to report healthcare services and procedures.

They are commonly used for claims, reimbursement, healthcare records, and communication between providers and payers.

Major CPT Sections

Category I CPT codes are organized into major sections, including:

  • Evaluation and Management
  • Anesthesia
  • Surgery
  • Radiology
  • Pathology and Laboratory
  • Medicine
Important: Each CPT section contains instructions and guidelines that should be reviewed when selecting codes.

CPT vs. ICD-10-CM

One of the easiest ways to remember the difference is:

ICD-10-CM = Why

It describes the patient's diagnosis or condition.

CPT = What

It describes the service or procedure performed.

Quick Check

A patient visits a healthcare provider because of a medical condition. The provider performs a service during the visit.

Which coding system is generally used to report the provider's service?

Answer: CPT is generally used to report the healthcare service or procedure performed.
