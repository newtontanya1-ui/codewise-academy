Lesson 3: Supplies and Durable Medical Equipment

Learn how HCPCS Level II codes are used to report medical supplies and durable medical equipment.

What Is Durable Medical Equipment?

Durable medical equipment is commonly abbreviated as DME.

DME includes certain types of medical equipment that may be used by patients for medical purposes, including equipment intended for use in the home when applicable coverage requirements are met.

Examples of Medical Equipment

Depending on the circumstances, HCPCS Level II codes may identify equipment such as:

  • Wheelchairs
  • Walkers
  • Hospital beds
  • Oxygen equipment
  • Blood glucose monitoring equipment
  • Other qualifying medical equipment
Remember: The exact code depends on the specific equipment documented and the complete HCPCS code description.

Medical Supplies

HCPCS Level II also contains codes for many medical and surgical supplies.

Examples may include:

  • Dressings
  • Catheter supplies
  • Diabetic supplies
  • Ostomy supplies
  • Other medical or surgical supplies

E Codes

Many durable medical equipment codes are found in the HCPCS Level II E-code range.

Example format:

E1234

This demonstrates the HCPCS Level II format of one letter followed by four numbers.

Important: Do not select a code based only on the letter. Always verify the complete code description.

Rental vs. Purchase

Some durable medical equipment may be rented, purchased, or handled under other payment rules.

The coder or biller should review the documentation, applicable HCPCS modifiers, and payer requirements when determining how the equipment should be reported.

Medical Necessity

Documentation should support why the equipment or supply is medically necessary for the patient.

Coverage requirements can vary depending on the item and payer.

CodeWise Tip: A valid HCPCS code does not automatically mean an item will be covered. Coverage and coding are related but separate considerations.

Documentation Matters

Accurate DME and supply coding may require details such as:

  • The exact item provided
  • Type or model when relevant
  • Quantity
  • Size when applicable
  • Whether the item was rented or purchased
  • The patient's medical need for the item

Quick Check

What does the abbreviation DME mean?

Answer: Durable Medical Equipment.

Lesson 3 Goals

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

  • Explain what DME stands for.
  • Recognize examples of durable medical equipment.
  • Recognize examples of medical supplies.
  • Understand the basic purpose of HCPCS E codes.
  • Recognize that rental and purchase rules may differ.
  • Explain why medical necessity matters.
  • Understand the importance of complete documentation.
