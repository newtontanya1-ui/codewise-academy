Lesson 5: Claims and Claim Forms

Learn the basic purpose of healthcare claims and the claim forms used to report services to insurance payers.

What Is a Healthcare Claim?

A healthcare claim is a request for payment that contains information about the patient, provider, diagnoses, services, procedures, and other billing details.

Claims are submitted to insurance payers so they can review and process the services reported.

Professional Claims

Professional claims are commonly used for services provided by physicians and other healthcare professionals.

The CMS-1500 form is commonly associated with professional healthcare claims.

Remember: Many claims are submitted electronically, but understanding the basic claim form helps billers understand the information required.

Institutional Claims

Institutional claims are commonly used by facilities such as hospitals and certain other healthcare organizations.

The UB-04 form is commonly associated with institutional claims.

Information Found on a Claim

A healthcare claim may include:

  • Patient information
  • Insurance information
  • Provider information
  • Dates of service
  • Diagnosis codes
  • Procedure or service codes
  • Modifiers when applicable
  • Charges
  • Units or quantities

Clean Claims

A clean claim generally contains the information needed for the payer to process the claim without avoidable errors or missing data.

Accurate and complete claims can help reduce delays and rejections.

Important: A claim should accurately reflect the documented services. Information should never be changed simply to obtain payment.

Electronic Claims

Many healthcare claims are submitted electronically.

Electronic claim systems can help check formatting and transmit claim data to the appropriate payer.

Claims may pass through a clearinghouse before reaching the insurance company.

What Is a Clearinghouse?

A healthcare clearinghouse can help receive, review, format, and route electronic claims between providers and payers.

Example:

A billing office sends an electronic claim to a clearinghouse.

The clearinghouse checks the claim for certain errors and then forwards it to the appropriate payer.

Common Claim Errors

Claims can have problems when information is missing or incorrect.

Common examples include:

  • Incorrect patient information
  • Incorrect insurance identification
  • Missing provider information
  • Invalid or incomplete codes
  • Missing modifiers when required
  • Incorrect dates of service
  • Incorrect units or quantities

Quick Check

Which claim form is commonly associated with professional claims?

Answer: The CMS-1500 form.

Lesson 5 Goals

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

  • Explain the purpose of a healthcare claim.
  • Recognize professional and institutional claims.
  • Identify the CMS-1500 and UB-04 forms.
  • Recognize common information found on claims.
  • Understand the basic meaning of a clean claim.
  • Explain the basic role of a clearinghouse.
  • Recognize common claim errors.
