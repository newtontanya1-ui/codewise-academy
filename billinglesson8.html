Lesson 8: Claim Denials and Rejections

Learn the difference between rejected and denied claims, common causes, and how medical billers respond to claim problems.

Rejected Claims

A rejected claim generally contains an error that prevents it from being accepted for processing.

The claim may need to be corrected and resubmitted before the payer can process it.

Remember: A rejected claim usually has not completed the payer's adjudication process.

Common Reasons for Rejections

Claims may be rejected because of problems such as:

  • Missing patient information
  • Incorrect insurance identification number
  • Invalid claim formatting
  • Missing required information
  • Invalid provider information
  • Incorrect or incomplete claim data

Denied Claims

A denied claim has generally been received and processed by the payer, but payment was not approved for some or all of the claim.

The payer normally provides information explaining why the claim was denied.

Common Reasons for Denials

Claim denials can occur for many reasons.

  • Service not covered
  • Medical necessity requirements not met
  • Missing prior authorization
  • Incorrect coding
  • Duplicate claim or service
  • Timely filing limits exceeded
  • Eligibility or coverage issues
  • Missing documentation

Rejection vs. Denial

Rejected Claim: The claim has an error that prevents it from being accepted for processing.

Denied Claim: The payer processed the claim but determined that payment should not be made for some or all of the claim.

Correcting a Rejected Claim

When a claim is rejected, the billing staff should identify the reason for the rejection.

The incorrect or missing information may then be corrected and the claim resubmitted according to applicable procedures.

Important: Only correct information that can be supported. Never invent information just to make a claim pass through the billing system.

Working a Denied Claim

When a claim is denied, the biller should review the payer's denial information and determine the appropriate next step.

This may involve:

  • Reviewing the original claim
  • Checking the patient's coverage
  • Reviewing coding information
  • Checking authorization requirements
  • Reviewing documentation
  • Correcting an error when appropriate
  • Submitting additional information
  • Appealing when appropriate

What Is an Appeal?

An appeal is a request for a payer to reconsider a claim decision.

Appeals should follow the payer's requirements and include appropriate supporting information when necessary.

Timely Filing

Payers may establish deadlines for submitting claims and certain appeals.

Medical billing staff should understand applicable timely filing requirements because missing a deadline can affect reimbursement.

Example

A claim is returned because the patient's insurance identification number is missing.

The billing staff verifies the correct identification number, updates the claim, and resubmits it.

This is an example of correcting a rejected claim.

Quick Check

What is the basic difference between a rejected claim and a denied claim?

Answer:

A rejected claim generally has not been accepted for full processing because of an error.

A denied claim has generally been processed, but payment was not approved for some or all of the claim.

Lesson 8 Goals

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

  • Explain what a rejected claim is.
  • Explain what a denied claim is.
  • Describe the difference between rejection and denial.
  • Recognize common causes of claim problems.
  • Understand the basic process for correcting rejected claims.
  • Explain the basic purpose of an appeal.
  • Understand why timely filing requirements matter.
