Lesson 6: Claim Submission and Adjudication

Learn what happens after a healthcare claim is prepared and submitted to an insurance payer.

Claim Submission

After a healthcare claim has been reviewed for accuracy and completeness, it is submitted to the payer for processing.

Many claims are submitted electronically through billing software or a healthcare clearinghouse.

Electronic Claim Processing

Electronic claims can move through several systems before they reach the payer.

A clearinghouse may review a claim for certain formatting or data errors before forwarding it to the insurance company.

Remember: A claim can be stopped before payer processing if required information is missing or invalid.

What Is Adjudication?

Claim adjudication is the process a payer uses to review a healthcare claim and determine how it should be processed.

During adjudication, the payer may review information such as:

  • Patient eligibility
  • Coverage
  • Provider information
  • Diagnosis codes
  • Procedure or service codes
  • Medical necessity
  • Contract and benefit rules

Possible Claim Outcomes

After adjudication, a claim may result in different outcomes.

  • Paid in full according to the allowed amount
  • Partially paid
  • Denied
  • Applied to patient responsibility
  • Returned for additional information

Allowed Amount

The allowed amount is the amount a payer recognizes for a covered healthcare service according to applicable plan or contract rules.

The provider's original charge and the payer's allowed amount may not be the same.

Patient Responsibility

After a claim is processed, part of the allowed amount may be assigned to the patient.

Patient responsibility may include:

  • Deductible
  • Copayment
  • Coinsurance
  • Other amounts determined by the health plan

Claim Status

Medical billers may monitor claim status after submission to determine whether the claim was received and processed.

If the claim has not been processed within the expected timeframe, follow-up may be necessary.

Example

A provider submits a claim for a healthcare service.

The payer reviews the patient's coverage, the reported services, applicable benefit rules, and other claim information.

The payer then determines the allowed amount, payment, and any patient responsibility.

Quick Check

What is the process called when an insurance payer reviews and processes a healthcare claim?

Answer: Claim adjudication.

Lesson 6 Goals

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

  • Explain the basic claim submission process.
  • Understand the role of electronic claims and clearinghouses.
  • Explain what claim adjudication means.
  • Recognize possible claim outcomes.
  • Understand the basic meaning of allowed amount.
  • Recognize common forms of patient responsibility.
  • Explain why claim status follow-up may be necessary.
