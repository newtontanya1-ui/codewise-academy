Lesson 3: Evaluation and Management

Learn the basic purpose of Evaluation and Management services and important concepts used when selecting E/M codes.

What Is Evaluation and Management?

Evaluation and Management services are commonly called E/M services.

These codes are used to report certain professional services involving the evaluation and management of a patient.

Types of E/M Services

E/M codes are used in several healthcare settings and types of encounters.

Examples include:

  • Office and other outpatient visits
  • Hospital inpatient and observation care
  • Emergency department visits
  • Nursing facility services
  • Home or residence services

New and Established Patients

Some E/M code families distinguish between new and established patients.

The applicable CPT definitions and guidelines should be reviewed before choosing a code.

Remember: Do not choose an E/M code simply because a patient personally considers a visit "new." CPT definitions and the circumstances of the encounter determine the appropriate classification.

Medical Decision Making

Medical decision making is commonly abbreviated as MDM.

For many E/M services, the level of service may be selected using the level of medical decision making when permitted by the applicable CPT rules.

MDM considers elements involving:

  • The number and complexity of problems addressed
  • The amount and complexity of data reviewed and analyzed
  • The risk of patient management

Using Time

Time may also be used to select certain E/M services when allowed by the applicable CPT guidelines.

Coders must review the requirements for the specific E/M code family because time rules can vary.

Important: Never select an E/M level based only on assumptions. The documentation must support the reported service.

Documentation

Provider documentation is essential when assigning an E/M code.

The coder should review the medical record and applicable CPT guidelines to determine which code accurately represents the documented service.

Example

A patient is seen for an office visit.

Before selecting the E/M code, the coder should identify the appropriate code family and review the documentation, CPT definitions, and applicable selection requirements.

Quick Check

What does the abbreviation MDM mean in E/M coding?

Answer: Medical Decision Making.

Lesson 3 Goals

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

  • Explain what E/M means.
  • Recognize common types of E/M services.
  • Understand that some code families distinguish new and established patients.
  • Explain what MDM stands for.
  • Recognize the three elements considered in MDM.
  • Understand that time may be used for certain E/M services.
  • Explain why provider documentation is important.
