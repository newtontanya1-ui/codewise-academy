Lesson 9: Symptoms, Signs, and Uncertain Diagnoses

Learn the basic ICD-10-CM rules for reporting symptoms, signs, and diagnoses that have not been confirmed.

Symptoms and Signs

Symptoms and signs may be reported when the healthcare provider has not established a definitive diagnosis.

Examples can include symptoms such as nausea, dizziness, abdominal pain, or headache.

Symptoms With a Confirmed Diagnosis

Signs and symptoms that are routinely associated with a confirmed diagnosis generally are not coded separately unless the classification instructs otherwise.

Remember: Always review the documentation and ICD-10-CM guidelines before deciding whether a symptom should be separately coded.

Outpatient Uncertain Diagnoses

In outpatient settings, diagnoses documented as probable, suspected, questionable, rule out, or similar terms are generally not coded as though they have been confirmed.

Instead, code the condition or conditions to the highest degree of certainty for that encounter, such as symptoms, signs, abnormal test results, or another documented reason for the visit.

Inpatient Uncertain Diagnoses

Certain inpatient settings have different guidelines for uncertain diagnoses documented at discharge.

Coders must determine the setting and follow the applicable ICD-10-CM Official Guidelines for Coding and Reporting.

Important: Do not apply inpatient uncertain-diagnosis rules to outpatient encounters.

Quick Check

An outpatient provider documents a suspected condition but does not confirm the diagnosis. The patient has a documented symptom related to the visit.

Should you automatically code the suspected condition as confirmed?

Answer: No. In the outpatient setting, an uncertain diagnosis generally is not coded as confirmed. Code to the highest degree of certainty supported by the encounter.

Lesson 9 Goals

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

  • Understand when symptoms and signs may be coded.
  • Recognize symptoms associated with a confirmed diagnosis.
  • Understand the basic outpatient uncertain-diagnosis rule.
  • Recognize that inpatient rules can be different.
  • Understand why the healthcare setting matters.
