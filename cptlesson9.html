Lesson 9: CPT Modifiers

Learn how CPT modifiers provide additional information about services and procedures.

What Is a CPT Modifier?

A modifier is added to a CPT code to provide additional information about a service or procedure.

A modifier can explain special circumstances without changing the basic definition of the CPT code.

Remember: A modifier should only be reported when the documentation and applicable coding rules support its use.

Why Are Modifiers Used?

Modifiers may communicate that a service or procedure was performed under circumstances that require additional explanation.

Depending on the situation, a modifier may provide information about:

  • A professional or technical component
  • A distinct procedural service
  • Multiple procedures
  • A discontinued procedure
  • A repeat procedure or service
  • Other circumstances affecting the service

Common Modifier Examples

Modifier 25

May identify a significant, separately identifiable E/M service by the same physician or other qualified healthcare professional on the same day as another procedure or service, when requirements are met.

Modifier 26

Identifies the professional component of certain services when applicable.

Modifier 50

May identify a bilateral procedure when applicable and supported by coding and payer rules.

Modifier 51

May be used to identify multiple procedures in applicable circumstances.

Modifier 59

May identify a distinct procedural service when appropriate and when documentation supports separate reporting.

Modifier TC

Identifies the technical component of certain services when applicable.

Modifier 25

Modifier 25 is associated with certain E/M services.

Documentation must support that the E/M service meets the requirements for separate reporting in addition to the other procedure or service.

Important: Do not automatically add modifier 25 simply because an E/M service and another procedure occurred on the same date.

Modifier 59

Modifier 59 can be used in appropriate circumstances to indicate that services that might normally be considered together were distinct.

Documentation must support the circumstances that justify separate reporting.

Documentation Matters

Modifiers should never be added simply to increase reimbursement or bypass a claim edit.

The medical record must support the modifier, and coders should follow current CPT instructions and applicable payer rules.

Quick Check

Which modifier identifies the professional component of certain services when applicable?

Answer: Modifier 26.

Lesson 9 Goals

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

  • Explain the basic purpose of CPT modifiers.
  • Understand that modifiers provide additional information.
  • Recognize modifiers 25, 26, 50, 51, 59, and TC.
  • Understand why documentation must support modifier use.
  • Recognize that payer requirements may affect modifier reporting.
