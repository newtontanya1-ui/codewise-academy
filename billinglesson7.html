Lesson 7: Payments, EOBs, and ERAs

Learn how insurance payments and claim decisions are communicated to healthcare providers and patients.

After a Claim Is Processed

After an insurance payer adjudicates a claim, information is sent explaining how the claim was processed.

This information can show payment amounts, adjustments, denials, and patient responsibility.

What Is an EOB?

EOB stands for Explanation of Benefits.

An EOB explains how a health insurance claim was processed.

It may include:

  • Services billed
  • Provider charges
  • Allowed amount
  • Amount paid by insurance
  • Adjustments
  • Patient responsibility
Remember: An EOB is generally not a bill. It explains how the insurance claim was processed.

What Is an ERA?

ERA stands for Electronic Remittance Advice.

An ERA is an electronic explanation sent to healthcare providers that provides information about claim payments and adjustments.

It can help billing staff post payments and identify claims requiring additional follow-up.

EOB vs. ERA

EOB: Commonly explains claim processing information to the patient.

ERA: Electronically communicates payment and adjustment information to the provider.

Payment Posting

Payment posting is the process of recording payments and adjustments on patient accounts.

Billing staff should carefully compare payment information with the claim and payer response.

Patient Responsibility

A payer may assign part of the allowed amount to the patient according to the patient's health plan.

Patient responsibility may include:

  • Deductible
  • Copayment
  • Coinsurance
  • Other applicable amounts

Adjustments

An adjustment changes the balance of an account based on payer contracts, claim processing, or other applicable billing rules.

Billers should understand why an adjustment occurred before posting it to an account.

Reviewing Payer Responses

When reviewing an EOB or ERA, billing staff may need to check:

  • Whether the claim was paid
  • How much was allowed
  • How much the payer paid
  • Whether adjustments were made
  • Whether an amount was assigned to the patient
  • Whether the claim was denied
CodeWise Tip: Do not assume every unpaid amount should automatically be billed to the patient. Review the payer's explanation first.

Example

A provider charges $200 for a service.

The payer's allowed amount is $150.

The payer pays $120 and assigns $30 to the patient's coinsurance.

The billing office reviews the payer response and posts the payment and applicable amounts to the patient's account.

Lesson 7 Goals

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

  • Explain what an EOB is.
  • Explain what an ERA is.
  • Recognize the difference between an EOB and ERA.
  • Understand the basic purpose of payment posting.
  • Recognize common forms of patient responsibility.
  • Understand why payer adjustments should be reviewed.
  • Identify important information on payer responses.
