Lesson 9: Medical Necessity and Documentation

Learn why medical necessity and complete documentation are essential when reporting HCPCS Level II codes.

What Is Medical Necessity?

Medical necessity generally refers to whether a healthcare item or service is reasonable and necessary for the patient's condition under applicable coverage requirements.

A HCPCS Level II code identifies an item or service, but the existence of a code does not automatically mean the item or service will be covered.

Why Documentation Matters

Documentation provides evidence of what was supplied, administered, or performed.

It can also help explain why the patient needed the item or service.

Remember: The code reported should accurately represent the documented item or service.

Important Documentation

Depending on the HCPCS code, documentation may need to include:

  • The item or service provided
  • The patient's condition
  • Quantity or number of units
  • Dosage when applicable
  • Laterality when applicable
  • Equipment specifications when relevant
  • Reason the item or service was needed

Diagnosis Coding

Diagnosis codes can help communicate the patient's condition or reason for receiving an item or service.

The diagnosis information should be supported by the medical record and reported according to applicable coding guidelines.

Coding and Coverage Are Not the Same

Correct coding and insurance coverage are related, but they are not the same thing.

A code may accurately describe an item or service even when a particular payer does not cover it under a patient's plan or circumstances.

CodeWise Tip: Always distinguish between selecting the correct code and determining whether a payer will cover the item or service.

Payer Requirements

Different payers may have specific coverage, billing, modifier, documentation, or authorization requirements.

Coders and billers should review applicable payer policies when necessary.

Avoid Assumptions

Never guess missing clinical information simply to select a HCPCS code.

If required information is missing or unclear, follow the organization's procedures for obtaining clarification.

Important: Accurate coding must be based on supported documentation, not assumptions.

Example

A patient receives durable medical equipment.

The coder should verify the exact equipment provided, review the documentation supporting the patient's need for it, and confirm the applicable HCPCS code and reporting requirements.

Quick Check

Does having a valid HCPCS Level II code automatically guarantee that an insurance company will cover the item or service?

Answer: No. Correct coding does not automatically guarantee coverage or payment.

Lesson 9 Goals

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

  • Explain the basic concept of medical necessity.
  • Understand why documentation supports HCPCS coding.
  • Recognize important documentation elements.
  • Understand the role of diagnosis information.
  • Explain the difference between correct coding and coverage.
  • Recognize that payer requirements may differ.
  • Understand why coders should never guess missing information.
