Lesson 4: Health Insurance Basics

Learn the basic health insurance terms medical billers need to understand, including deductibles, copayments, coinsurance, premiums, networks, and patient responsibility.

What Is Health Insurance?

Health insurance helps pay for covered healthcare services according to the rules of a patient's insurance plan.

The amount paid by the insurance company and the amount owed by the patient depend on the plan, covered services, and other benefit rules.

Premium

A premium is the amount paid to maintain health insurance coverage.

Premiums may be paid monthly or according to another schedule established by the plan.

Remember: A premium is different from a deductible, copayment, or coinsurance amount.

Deductible

A deductible is an amount the patient may need to pay for covered healthcare services before the insurance plan begins paying according to the plan's benefit rules.

Example:

If a patient has a $1,000 deductible, certain covered services may be applied toward that deductible before the plan begins paying its share.

Copayment

A copayment, often called a copay, is a fixed amount a patient may owe for a covered healthcare service.

Example:

A patient's plan may require a $30 copayment for an office visit.

Coinsurance

Coinsurance is a percentage of the allowed amount that the patient may be responsible for after applicable plan requirements are met.

Example:

If the allowed amount is $200 and the patient has 20% coinsurance, the patient's share would be $40 in this simplified example.

Allowed Amount

The allowed amount is the amount recognized by the payer for a covered service according to applicable contract or plan rules.

The provider's billed charge may be different from the payer's allowed amount.

In-Network and Out-of-Network

A provider may be considered in-network when the provider participates with the patient's insurance plan.

Out-of-network services may be subject to different benefit rules, higher patient costs, or limited coverage depending on the plan.

Important: Network rules vary by insurance plan, so billers should verify current payer information when needed.

Patient Responsibility

Patient responsibility is the portion of the healthcare cost assigned to the patient after the payer processes the claim.

It may include:

  • Deductible
  • Copayment
  • Coinsurance
  • Noncovered amounts when applicable
  • Other amounts determined by the health plan

Medicare and Medicaid

Medicare and Medicaid are government healthcare programs with different eligibility, coverage, billing, and payment requirements.

Medical billers should follow the current rules that apply to the patient's specific coverage.

Why Insurance Knowledge Matters

Medical billers need to understand basic insurance terminology so they can correctly review claims, payments, and patient balances.

Insurance information should always be verified when necessary because benefits and coverage can vary from one plan to another.

Quick Check

Which term describes a percentage of an allowed amount that the patient may be responsible for?

Answer: Coinsurance.

Lesson 4 Goals

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

  • Explain what a health insurance premium is.
  • Explain what a deductible is.
  • Recognize a copayment.
  • Explain the basic meaning of coinsurance.
  • Understand the meaning of allowed amount.
  • Recognize in-network and out-of-network concepts.
  • Understand common forms of patient responsibility.
