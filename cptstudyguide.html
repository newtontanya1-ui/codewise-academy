CPT Basics

  • CPT stands for Current Procedural Terminology.
  • CPT codes are mainly used to report medical procedures and services.
  • Most CPT codes contain 5 numbers.
  • CPT codes are maintained by the American Medical Association.

Main CPT Sections

  • Evaluation and Management: Office, hospital, and other patient visits.
  • Anesthesia: Anesthesia services for procedures.
  • Surgery: Surgical procedures organized by body system.
  • Radiology: X-rays, CT scans, MRIs, and other imaging.
  • Pathology and Laboratory: Laboratory and pathology testing.
  • Medicine: Services such as injections, vaccines, dialysis, and cardiology procedures.

CPT Categories

  • Category I: Standard procedures and services.
  • Category II: Performance measurement tracking codes.
  • Category III: Temporary codes for emerging technology and procedures.

Modifiers

  • Modifiers provide extra information about a procedure or service.
  • Modifier 25: Significant, separately identifiable E/M service.
  • Modifier 50: Bilateral procedure.
  • Modifier 51: Multiple procedures.
  • Modifier 59: Distinct procedural service.

Helpful CPT Coding Tips

  • Start your search in the CPT Index.
  • Always verify the code in the main CPT section.
  • Read all notes and guidelines before selecting a code.
  • Check whether a modifier is required.
  • Pay attention to whether a procedure is unilateral or bilateral.
  • Do not choose a CPT code based only on the code description in the Index.
