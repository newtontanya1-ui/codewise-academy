Lesson 9: Accounts Receivable and Follow-Up

Learn how medical billing staff monitor unpaid balances and follow up on outstanding healthcare claims.

What Is Accounts Receivable?

Accounts receivable, often called A/R, represents money that is owed to a healthcare provider for services that have been provided but have not yet been fully paid.

These balances may involve insurance payers, patients, or other responsible parties.

Why A/R Follow-Up Matters

Submitting a claim does not always mean payment will automatically be received.

Medical billing staff monitor outstanding claims to identify payment delays and other problems.

Remember: Regular follow-up can help identify claim issues before important payer deadlines are missed.

A/R Aging

Accounts receivable balances are often organized according to how long they have been outstanding.

Common aging categories may include:

  • 0–30 days
  • 31–60 days
  • 61–90 days
  • 91–120 days
  • More than 120 days

Aging reports help billing staff identify older balances that may require additional attention.

Insurance Follow-Up

When an insurance claim remains unpaid, billing staff may review the claim status.

Follow-up may include checking:

  • Whether the payer received the claim
  • Whether the claim is still processing
  • Whether additional information is needed
  • Whether the claim was rejected
  • Whether the claim was denied
  • Whether payment has already been issued

Patient Balances

After insurance processes a claim, certain amounts may become the patient's responsibility.

These may include applicable:

  • Deductibles
  • Copayments
  • Coinsurance
  • Other patient-responsibility amounts
Important: Billing staff should review the payer response before determining what amount should be billed to the patient.

Documenting Follow-Up

Billing staff should document important follow-up activity on an account.

Notes may include:

  • Date of follow-up
  • Claim status
  • Information received from the payer
  • Actions taken
  • Additional information requested
  • Next follow-up date when appropriate

Timely Filing

Insurance payers may establish deadlines for submitting claims, corrected claims, and appeals.

Monitoring accounts receivable can help billing staff identify issues before applicable deadlines are missed.

Example

A claim has remained unpaid for 45 days.

The biller checks the payer's system and discovers that additional information is required.

The biller follows the appropriate process, documents the action taken, and continues monitoring the claim.
