Lesson 5: Ambulance and Transportation Services

Learn the basics of HCPCS Level II coding for ambulance and certain transportation services.

Ambulance Services

HCPCS Level II contains codes used to report certain ambulance transportation services.

Code selection depends on the type and level of transportation service actually provided.

Types of Ambulance Transportation

Ambulance transportation may involve different levels and methods of service.

Examples may include:

  • Basic Life Support (BLS)
  • Advanced Life Support (ALS)
  • Specialty care transport
  • Ground ambulance transportation
  • Air ambulance transportation
Remember: The documentation must support the level and type of ambulance service reported.

Origin and Destination

Ambulance claims may require information about where the patient was picked up and where the patient was transported.

HCPCS modifiers may be used to provide information about the origin and destination of an ambulance trip.

Example:

A patient may be transported from a residence to a hospital.

The applicable coding and payer rules determine how the origin and destination are reported.

Mileage

Ambulance mileage may be reported separately when appropriate.

Documentation should support the mileage and transportation service being billed.

Important: Always follow current HCPCS instructions and payer requirements when reporting ambulance mileage.

Medical Necessity

Medical necessity is especially important when reporting ambulance services.

The documentation should support why ambulance transportation was medically necessary for the patient.

Simply transporting a patient does not automatically mean that an ambulance service meets a payer's coverage requirements.

Documentation Matters

Important ambulance documentation may include:

  • Patient's condition
  • Reason transportation was required
  • Level of service provided
  • Origin of the trip
  • Destination of the trip
  • Mileage when applicable
  • Services provided during transport

Quick Check

What two locations are important when reporting an ambulance trip?

Answer: The patient's origin and destination.

Lesson 5 Goals

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

  • Explain the basic purpose of ambulance HCPCS coding.
  • Recognize BLS and ALS terminology.
  • Understand why origin and destination matter.
  • Recognize that mileage may be separately reportable.
  • Explain why medical necessity is important.
  • Understand the importance of complete documentation.
