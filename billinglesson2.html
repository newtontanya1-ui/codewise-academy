Lesson 2: The Healthcare Revenue Cycle

Learn how healthcare services move through the billing process from patient registration to final payment.

What Is the Revenue Cycle?

The healthcare revenue cycle is the financial process used to manage a patient account from the beginning of an encounter through final payment and account resolution.

It includes many steps involving registration, documentation, coding, billing, claim processing, payment, and follow-up.

Step 1: Patient Registration

The revenue cycle often begins when a patient schedules or arrives for healthcare services.

Important information may include:

  • Patient name
  • Date of birth
  • Address
  • Insurance information
  • Contact information
Remember: Incorrect registration information can cause problems later in the billing process.

Step 2: Insurance Verification

Insurance information may be checked before services are provided.

Verification can help determine whether coverage is active and identify important benefit information.

Step 3: Healthcare Services

The patient receives healthcare services, and the provider documents the encounter.

Complete documentation is important because it supports coding, billing, and the medical record.

Step 4: Medical Coding

Diagnoses, procedures, services, and supplies are translated into standardized medical codes when appropriate.

These codes help communicate what happened during the patient's encounter.

Step 5: Claim Creation

The biller uses patient, insurance, provider, coding, and service information to prepare the healthcare claim.

The claim should be reviewed for completeness and accuracy before submission.

Step 6: Claim Submission

The completed claim is submitted to the payer for processing.

Claims may be transmitted electronically through systems that help check and route claim information.

Step 7: Claim Adjudication

Adjudication is the process the payer uses to review and process the claim.

The payer may determine:

  • Whether the service is covered
  • How much is allowed
  • How much the payer will pay
  • Whether the patient owes a portion
  • Whether the claim needs additional review

Step 8: Payment and Follow-Up

After the payer processes the claim, payment and claim information are returned to the provider.

If a claim is unpaid, rejected, denied, or partially paid, the billing team may need to investigate and follow up.

Why the Revenue Cycle Matters

Each step affects the steps that follow. An error early in the process can create delays or payment problems later.

Example:

If a patient's insurance information is entered incorrectly during registration, the claim may be rejected when it is submitted.

Quick Check

What is the name of the process a payer uses to review and process a healthcare claim?

Answer: Claim adjudication.

Lesson 2 Goals

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

  • Explain the healthcare revenue cycle.
  • Recognize the major steps in the billing process.
  • Explain why patient registration matters.
  • Understand the role of medical coding in the revenue cycle.
  • Explain what claim adjudication means.
  • Understand why payment follow-up is important.
