Lesson 3: Patient Registration and Insurance Verification

Learn why accurate patient information and insurance verification are important before healthcare claims are submitted.

Patient Registration

Patient registration is one of the first steps in the medical billing process.

Accurate registration helps ensure that the patient's account and future insurance claim contain the correct information.

Important Patient Information

Registration information may include:

  • Full legal name
  • Date of birth
  • Address
  • Phone number
  • Insurance information
  • Subscriber information
  • Relationship to the subscriber
Remember: Even small errors in a patient's name, date of birth, or insurance identification number can cause claim problems.

What Is Insurance Verification?

Insurance verification is the process of checking a patient's insurance information before or around the time healthcare services are provided.

The goal is to confirm that the information on file is accurate and determine important benefit and coverage information when available.

Information That May Be Verified

Depending on the payer and service, verification may include:

  • Whether coverage is active
  • Effective dates
  • Member or subscriber identification
  • Copayment information
  • Deductible information
  • Coinsurance information
  • Referral requirements
  • Prior authorization requirements

Primary and Secondary Insurance

Some patients have more than one insurance plan.

When this happens, it may be necessary to determine which payer is primary and which payer is secondary.

Coordination of benefits rules can help determine the order in which claims are submitted.

Prior Authorization

Some healthcare services require authorization from the payer before the service is provided.

Prior authorization does not automatically guarantee payment, but failure to obtain required authorization can create billing or coverage problems.

Important: Verification of benefits and authorization requirements should follow the payer's current rules.

Why Verification Matters

Insurance verification can help reduce avoidable billing problems.

Problems that may occur when information is incorrect or incomplete include:

  • Claim rejection
  • Claim denial
  • Billing the wrong payer
  • Incorrect patient responsibility
  • Delays in reimbursement

Example

A patient's insurance card shows a different member identification number than the number stored in the billing system.

The information should be reviewed and corrected before the claim is submitted.

Quick Check

Why is it important to verify a patient's insurance information?

Answer:

Verification helps confirm accurate coverage information and can reduce avoidable claim and billing problems.

Lesson 3 Goals

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

  • Explain why accurate patient registration matters.
  • Identify common patient registration information.
  • Explain the purpose of insurance verification.
  • Recognize common insurance benefit information.
  • Understand the basic concept of primary and secondary insurance.
  • Recognize the importance of prior authorization requirements.
  • Explain how verification can help prevent billing problems.
