Lesson 1: Introduction to Medical Billing

Learn what medical billing is, what medical billers do, and how billing connects healthcare services with payment.

What Is Medical Billing?

Medical billing is the process of preparing, submitting, and following up on healthcare claims so providers can receive payment for services.

Medical billers work with information from the patient's medical record, insurance information, procedure codes, diagnosis codes, and payer requirements.

What Does a Medical Biller Do?

Medical billers may perform tasks such as:

  • Reviewing patient and insurance information
  • Checking claims for accuracy
  • Submitting claims to insurance payers
  • Reviewing payment information
  • Working with rejected or denied claims
  • Following up on unpaid claims
  • Helping maintain accurate patient account balances

Medical Coding vs. Medical Billing

Medical coding and medical billing work closely together, but they are not exactly the same.

Medical coding converts documented diagnoses, procedures, services, and supplies into standardized codes.

Medical billing uses the coded information and other claim data to prepare and submit claims for reimbursement.

Easy way to remember:

Coding identifies what happened.

Billing helps communicate that information to the payer for payment processing.

Who Are the Payers?

A payer is an organization or program responsible for processing healthcare claims according to applicable coverage and payment rules.

Payers can include:

  • Private health insurance companies
  • Medicare
  • Medicaid
  • Other government healthcare programs

The Basic Billing Process

A simplified medical billing process may look like this:

  1. The patient receives healthcare services.
  2. The services and diagnoses are documented.
  3. Applicable codes and claim information are prepared.
  4. The claim is submitted to the payer.
  5. The payer processes or adjudicates the claim.
  6. Payment or other claim information is returned.
  7. Any remaining balance or claim issue is reviewed.

Why Accuracy Matters

Medical billing depends on accurate information. Errors can delay claim processing or contribute to rejections and denials.

Billers should carefully review information such as:

  • Patient name and identifying information
  • Insurance information
  • Dates of service
  • Diagnosis and procedure codes
  • Provider information
  • Required claim details
Remember: Never change or invent information simply to make a claim pay. Claims should accurately represent the documented services.

Quick Check

What is one of the main purposes of medical billing?

Answer:

To prepare, submit, and follow up on healthcare claims so providers can receive appropriate reimbursement for covered services.
