Lesson 5: Sequencing Codes

Learn how ICD-10-CM diagnosis codes are placed in the correct order when more than one condition is reported.

What Is Code Sequencing?

Code sequencing means placing diagnosis codes in the correct order on a claim or health record.

When a patient has more than one diagnosis, coding guidelines and the circumstances of the encounter help determine which diagnosis should be listed first.

First-Listed Diagnosis

In the outpatient setting, the first-listed diagnosis is generally the condition, problem, or other reason chiefly responsible for the services provided during the encounter.

Remember: Always follow the ICD-10-CM Official Guidelines for Coding and Reporting when determining code sequencing.

Principal Diagnosis

In the inpatient setting, the principal diagnosis is the condition established after study to be chiefly responsible for the patient's admission to the hospital.

Other conditions that meet reporting requirements may be assigned as additional diagnoses.

Code First

A "code first" instruction tells the coder that an underlying condition should be sequenced before the condition containing the instruction.

Tip: When you see a "code first" note, stop and check which condition must be reported before the other code.

Use Additional Code

A "use additional code" instruction indicates that another code may be needed to completely describe the patient's condition.

These instructional notes help determine which codes are needed and how they should be sequenced.

Why Sequencing Matters

Correct sequencing helps accurately communicate the main reason for the patient's encounter and any additional conditions that affected the patient's care.

Incorrect sequencing can result in inaccurate health information and may affect claim processing and reimbursement.

Quick Check

You see a "code first" instruction under an ICD-10-CM diagnosis code. What should you do?

Answer: Review the documentation and coding instructions to identify the underlying condition that must be sequenced first.

Lesson 5 Goals

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

  • Explain what code sequencing means.
  • Understand the purpose of a first-listed diagnosis.
  • Understand the basic meaning of principal diagnosis.
  • Recognize a "code first" instruction.
  • Recognize a "use additional code" instruction.
  • Explain why correct sequencing is important.
